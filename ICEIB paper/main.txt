AirGuard AI: An Evidence-Grounded Domain AI Agent for Station-Scale Short-Term
PM2.5 Forecasting and Conservative Outdoor Activity Decision Support

Ming-Hsien Tsai (7115056013), Hung-Yang Lin (7115056179), and Ping-Kun Hsu (8114056002)
AIoT Data Analytics and Applications (AIoT-DA) Research Group, Team 2
Taichung, Taiwan

Abstract—[A. Attention Getter / Motivation] Ambient air pollution remains a major
global environmental health hazard; the World Health Organization (WHO) estimates
that outdoor air pollution caused approximately 4.2 million premature deaths
worldwide in 2019, and while Taiwan's Ministry of Environment (MOENV) reports
that national annual mean fine particulate matter (PM2.5) declined from
16.2 ug/m^3 in 2019 to 12.8 ug/m^3 in 2025 (an approximate 21% long-term
reduction), macro-level annual averages cannot resolve station-specific,
hour-to-hour exposure risks when citizens schedule outdoor activities. [B. But /
Challenge] However, existing public monitoring platforms primarily display static
current conditions while leaving users to manually reconcile short-term
concentration trajectories, activity duration, population vulnerability, data
freshness, and predictive uncertainty; meanwhile, machine learning (ML) forecast
models frequently suffer from temporal data leakage, and unconstrained large
language models (LLMs) risk hallucinating environmental readings or conflating
single-pollutant predictions with official multi-pollutant Air Quality Index
(AQI) ratings. [C. Cure / Proposed Solution] To address these challenges, we
propose AirGuard AI, an evidence-grounded Domain AI Agent for station-scale
short-term PM2.5 forecasting and conservative outdoor activity decision support.
[D. Development / Method] Designed around six official MOENV monitoring stations
in Taichung City as a pilot domain, AirGuard AI integrates four official open
datasets (AQX_P_432 real-time AQI, AQX_P_13 hourly PM2.5 observations, AQX_P_488
historical records, and AQX_P_07 TWD97 station metadata) with versioned 2025
MOENV activity guidelines, benchmarking Ridge regression, Random Forest, and
XGBoost against Persistence and 24-hour Seasonal Naive baselines across 1-, 3-,
and 6-hour horizons while a tool-allowlisted ReAct planner agent orchestrates
deterministic tools, station-scale concentration-time exposure indexing
(ug*h/m^3), and fail-closed safety degradation. [E. Experiments / Evaluation] To
evaluate our proposed scheme, we report the completed Phase W1 empirical data
quality engineering and cryptographic SHA256 snapshot verification across
123,055 valid hourly observations from the six Taichung stations (2024-2026),
paired with a dual-track validation protocol combining chronological 70/15/15
rolling-origin backtesting (Track A) and a 30-scenario adversarial and boundary
agent test suite (Track B). [F. Findings / Expected Outcomes] Empirical Phase W1
results confirm 95.49%-97.90% station window coverage while resolving five
upstream data anomalies (duplicate daily rows, unobserved calendar days,
instrument ceiling thresholding, pagination timeouts, and missing publication-
delay logs), establishing an auditable station-scale decision prototype whose
final predictive skill scores and decision efficacy are governed by pre-
registered ongoing experiments without claiming street-level navigation, personal
inhaled dose, or clinical diagnosis.

Index Terms—Air Quality Index (AQI), Decision Support Systems, Domain AI Agent,
Explainable AI (SHAP), Internet of Things (IoT) Open Data, PM2.5 Time-Series
Forecasting, ReAct Tool Calling.

I. INTRODUCTION

A. General Current Status and Public Health Motivation (G)
With the rapid expansion of Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) sensing
infrastructure and open government environmental repositories, public access to
ambient air quality monitoring has improved substantially worldwide.
Nevertheless, outdoor air pollution persists as a critical public health threat.
According to the World Health Organization (WHO), approximately 99% of the
global population in 2019 lived in areas exceeding WHO Air Quality Guideline
(AQG) limits, resulting in roughly 4.2 million premature deaths annually [1],
[2]. In Taiwan, long-term air quality management documented by the Ministry of
Environment (MOENV) reduced the national annual mean concentration of fine
particulate matter (PM2.5) from 16.2 ug/m^3 in 2019 to 12.8 ug/m^3 in 2025,
representing an approximate 21% reduction [3], [4]. Taiwan's MOENV Open Data
Platform continuously disseminates station-level real-time Air Quality Index
(AQI) reports (AQX_P_432), daily updated hourly pollutant series (AQX_P_13),
historical AQI and wind archives (AQX_P_488), and TWD97 station coordinates
(AQX_P_07) [5]-[8], creating a rich empirical foundation for data-driven
environmental services without requiring proprietary sensor hardware.

B. However: Core Technical and Domain Challenges (H)
However, an encouraging national annual average operates on a fundamentally
different spatiotemporal scale than an individual's immediate daily activity
decision, such as selecting a 40-minute window over the next three to six hours
for outdoor jogging or commuting near a specific monitoring station in Taichung
City. Translating raw monitoring data into reliable personal activity guidance
faces four severe challenges:

1) Spatiotemporal and Timestamp Provenance Ambiguity: Existing platforms
routinely conflate observation time (observed_at), official release time
(published_at), and client fetch time (retrieved_at). Upstream sensor
maintenance or telemetry gaps produce missing hourly slots that naive pipelines
erroneously fill with 0 ug/m^3, falsely indicating pollution-free air.
Furthermore, point-source regulatory stations characterize neighborhood-scale
ambient concentrations and cannot independently substantiate street-by-street
clean-route rankings without co-located mobile sensors [9], [10].

2) Regulatory Divergence Between Official AQI and Single-Pollutant PM2.5: Under
Taiwan's MOENV AQI framework (including the revised sub-index concentration
breakpoints effective January 1, 2025), official AQI is the maximum sub-index
across six pollutants (O3, PM2.5, PM10, CO, SO2, and NO2) computed over distinct
moving-average windows [11]. Equating a single-pollutant raw hourly PM2.5
forecast to a "predicted official AQI" violates regulatory semantics.

3) Time-Series Leakage and the Accuracy-Decision Gap: Many PM2.5 forecasting
pipelines employ random train-test splitting or future-looking rolling features
that leak future distribution statistics, while omitting simple baselines such as
Persistence and Seasonal Naive [12]-[14]. Moreover, empirical evidence shows
that lower overall Mean Absolute Error (MAE) does not automatically guarantee
better outdoor scheduling decisions when candidate windows differ by less than
model uncertainty or when acute pollution spikes are smoothed [15].

4) Unconstrained Generative AI Risks: Deploying an unconstrained LLM chatbot over
environmental APIs invites numeric hallucination, silent substitution of stale
cache data as live observations, vulnerability to indirect prompt injection
embedded in external API payloads, and unauthorized medical advice [16]-[18].

C. In Literature and Judgement of Existing Paradigms (I, J)
In the literature (I), approaches to environmental decision support have evolved
from simple to complex across three major categories: 1) static open-data
dashboards and deterministic rule tables [5], [11]; 2) statistical, classical ML,
and deep spatiotemporal forecasting models paired with post-hoc explainable AI
(XAI) such as SHAP [12], [19], [21], [22], [24]; and 3) tool-augmented LLM
agents utilizing Reasoning and Acting (ReAct) or Instructor-Worker workflows
[16], [17], [23].

Evaluating the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT Judgement,
J) of these paradigms reveals a clear trade-off:
- Rule-Based Dashboards: Offer high regulatory fidelity and zero hallucination
  (Strength), but provide only backward-looking or current status without
  multi-horizon candidate window comparison (Weakness).
- Standalone ML/DL Forecasters: Capture nonlinear temporal autocorrelation across
  1-6 hour horizons (Strength), yet frequently lack strict rolling-origin
  baseline audits, conflate SHAP feature attribution with physical atmospheric
  causality, and fail to translate point errors into uncertainty-aware activity
  abstention (Weakness/Threat) [13], [15], [22].
- Autonomous LLM Agents: Excel at natural language intent parsing and multi-step
  tool orchestration (Opportunity), but remain brittle under missing/stale data
  and vulnerable to prompt injection or regulatory overclaiming when not bounded
  by deterministic domain guardrails (Threat) [17], [18].

D. Keypoints, Novelty, and Paper Organization (K, L)
To bridge this gap, the core design spirit and novelty (K) of AirGuard AI (v3
specification) lie in a three-tier separation of concerns: an allowlist-
constrained ReAct planner agent handles intent interpretation and tool routing,
version-locked leakage-safe ML models generate 1-, 3-, and 6-hour raw hourly
PM2.5 forecasts, and a deterministic domain rule engine enforces 2025 MOENV AQI
activity guidance, concentration-time exposure indexing, and fail-closed safety
degradation. The major contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

1) Reproducible MOENV Data Engineering and Cryptographic Provenance (Verified in
Phase W1): We implement and empirically audit a Python ingestion and quality-
control pipeline across four MOENV datasets for all six official stations in
Taichung City (123,055 valid hourly observations, 2024-2026), resolving duplicate
daily rows, trailing date whitespace, and unobserved calendar days while saving
raw paginated bytes with SHA256 manifests for deterministic offline replay.

2) Leakage-Safe Multi-Horizon PM2.5 Benchmarking Protocol: We design a
chronological 70/15/15 split and rolling-origin evaluation comparing Persistence
and 24-hour Seasonal Naive baselines against Ridge regression, Random Forest,
and XGBoost at h in {1, 3, 6} h on identical valid sample pairs, tagging
historical backtests without publication-delay logs as
reconstructed_from_observed_at.

3) Dual-Track Evidence Separation and Conservative Window Comparison: We
architect an eight-module pipeline that strictly separates official multi-
pollutant AQI from predicted hourly PM2.5 and station-scale concentration-time
exposure proxies (ug*h/m^3), abstaining from forced window rankings when
inter-window differences fall within validation error bounds.

4) Dual-Track Evaluation Matrix and Scope Governance: We couple quantitative
forecasting metrics (MAE, RMSE, Skill Score, decision regret) with a 30-scenario
agent verification suite testing tool accuracy, citation completeness,
regulatory compliance, prompt-injection defense, and fail-closed degradation.

Paper Organization (L): The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section II reviews related work across AI technology and environmental domain
know-how, synthesizing our Deep Research findings and structured SWOT comparison
table. Section III details the proposed AirGuard AI design following the
systematic PA-PM methodology. Section IV presents the Phase W1 empirical data
audit and the dual-track experimental evaluation framework. Section V concludes
the paper and outlines ongoing work.


II. RELATED WORK

In accordance with an interdisciplinary research methodology balancing
approximately 50% Artificial Intelligence technology and 50% environmental health
domain know-how, we categorize prior studies from foundational statistical and
regulatory methods to advanced machine learning and tool-augmented agent
frameworks.

A. Methods: AI Technology and Environmental Domain Know-How (M)
1) AI Technology—From Naive Baselines to Tree Ensembles, XAI, and ReAct Agents:
In time-series forecasting, classical baselines such as Persistence
(y_hat_{t+h} = y_t) and Seasonal Naive (y_hat_{t+h} = y_{t+h-24}) serve as
indispensable reference points. Large-scale empirical evidence from the M5
forecasting competition [20] and Hewamalage et al.'s forecast evaluation
taxonomy [13] demonstrate that complex ML models must be evaluated via rolling-
origin splits and skill scores relative to naive baselines to avoid illusory
gains. In air quality forecasting specifically, Chianese and Riccio [12]
synthesized 409 studies and concluded that no single architecture universally
dominates across horizons and monitoring regimes; rather, leakage-aware
validation [14] and feature availability at the forecast origin govern deployment
reliability. Joseph and Ganapathy [19] further observed that L2-regularized
linear models (Ridge) and tree ensembles (Random Forest, XGBoost) frequently
match deep recurrent or transformer models on tabular station histories while
providing superior computational efficiency. For model interpretability,
Lundberg and Lee's SHAP framework [21] is widely applied to tree models, yet
Houdou et al. [22] emphasized in a systematic review that SHAP quantifies
local/global model feature attribution rather than physical atmospheric
causality. At the interaction layer, Yao et al. [16] introduced ReAct to
interleave reasoning traces with external tool calls, Gao et al. [23]
demonstrated Instructor-Worker LLM coordination for wildfire air quality
analysis, and Nie and Liu [17] alongside OWASP LLM01 (2026) [18] established
that environmental LLMs must operate as rule-constrained assistants with strict
tool allowlists to prevent prompt injection and excessive agency.

2) Domain Know-How—Exposure Hierarchy, Regulatory AQI, and Decision Utility:
At the public health foundation, the WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines [1], [2]
define short- and long-term reference thresholds for criteria pollutants while
noting that population guidelines are not individual clinical diagnostic
criteria. In exposure science, Steinle et al. [9] formalized the distinction
among: 1) fixed-station ambient concentration C(t) (ug/m^3); 2) time-integrated
exposure proxy sum_i C(t_i)*Delta_t_i (ug*h/m^3); and 3) true personal inhaled
dose, which requires microenvironmental infiltration, street-canyon geometry,
and minute ventilation modeling. Both U.S. EPA sensor guidance [10] and Lo et
al.'s Taiwanese spatiotemporal PM2.5 study [24] confirm that sparse regulatory
stations cannot validate street-level clean-route navigation without independent
mobile or satellite-LUR calibration. Under Taiwan's regulatory standard, MOENV's
2025 revised AQI [11] aggregates six pollutants over pollutant-specific moving
averages into six tiers with differentiated guidance for general and sensitive
populations. Crucially, Berlinghieri et al. [15] evaluated six hourly PM2.5
forecast systems for daily outdoor planning ("whether to go out" vs. "when to go
out"), proving that lower point-forecast MAE does not monotonically yield
superior activity window selection.

B. New Proposed Trend, Deep Research Synthesis, and Organized Comparison (N, O)
Building on our Deep Research investigation (N), AirGuard AI is anchored by five
core domain mindsets (Q1): 1) Exposure context over static visualization;
2) Spatiotemporal fidelity and three-timestamp provenance; 3) Baseline-first and
leakage-safe validation; 4) Separation of point forecast accuracy and decision
utility; and 5) Human-centered, fail-closed agent guardrails. Furthermore, we
address three major professional controversies (Q2):
1) Complex Deep Learning vs. Simple Baselines: Rather than assuming deep neural
networks outperform simpler models on single-station series, we require Ridge,
Random Forest, and XGBoost to prove positive skill against Persistence and
24-hour Seasonal Naive on identical valid sample pairs.
2) Average Forecast Error vs. Actionable Activity Decisions: Because adjacent
candidate windows often differ by less than model validation error [15], we pair
point-error metrics (Track A) with uncertainty-overlap abstention and decision-
regret evaluation (Track B).
3) Autonomous Agent vs. Rule-Constrained Pipeline: To satisfy ten deep domain
reasoning checks (Q3: Why single-hour PM2.5 != official AQI; What-if API lags by
3 hours; Trade-off of marginal MAE gains; Failure cases of random splitting;
Evidence of rule compliance; Horizon stratification; Architectural separation;
Spatial station limits; Uncertainty crossing thresholds; and Non-causality of
SHAP), the LLM is restricted to an eight-tool allowlist with deterministic
output verification.

Table I organizes the representative literature and SWOT judgement (O),
establishing a clear four-stage progression: Existing Research provides isolated
open datasets, ML forecasters, and generic tool agents -> Limitations arise from
timestamp conflation, temporal leakage, AQI/PM2.5 confusion, and unconstrained
LLM hallucination -> Research Gap lies in the absence of an end-to-end,
cryptographically reproducible Taiwan MOENV station-scale framework uniting
leakage-safe 1/3/6-hour baselines, versioned 2025 AQI rules, and fail-closed
agent auditing -> Our Project (AirGuard AI) bridges this gap.


III. PROPOSED DESIGN

Following the systematic PA-PM design methodology, this section presents the
problem statement, data foundation, dual-track paradigm, eight-module
architecture, mathematical formulation, fail-closed flowchart, line-numbered
algorithms, concrete user scenarios, and deterministic verification bounds of
AirGuard AI.

A. Aim, Data Background, Dual-Track Cure, and Paradigm Shift (PA-PD)
Aim (PA): Given a user query Q specifying a target location/station in Taichung
City, an outdoor activity type, a planned duration Delta_t <= 6 h, a candidate
time window within the next 6 hours, and a population group g in {general,
sensitive}, AirGuard AI aims to generate an evidence-grounded, uncertainty-aware
decision response R_out that compares eligible activity windows while preserving
cryptographic data provenance and regulatory consistency.

Background and Open Data Foundation (PB): The system operates directly on four
official Taiwan MOENV open datasets (Table II) accessed via REST API v2
(https://data.moenv.gov.tw/api/v2/{DATASET}) with server-side key resolution
(MOENV_API_KEY) or SHA256-verified offline snapshots.

Dual-Track Cure and Cause (PC): Because official AQI is a regulatory multi-
pollutant composite index whereas short-term ML models predict single-pollutant
raw hourly PM2.5 (ug/m^3), AirGuard AI enforces a dual-track evidence
separation: current risk tiering is anchored strictly to official published AQI
(AQX_P_432) and 2025 MOENV activity rules, while temporal window comparison
across h in {1, 3, 6} h relies on version-locked PM2.5 forecasts and validation
error bands.

Design Paradigm Shift (PD): Unlike conventional dashboards that dump raw
pollutant tables or unconstrained chatbots that generate free-text health
advice, AirGuard AI shifts the paradigm to Planner-Analytics-Domain Rule
Decoupling: the LLM never invents numbers or overrides regulatory thresholds,
and every output card is bound to a verified tool trace.

B. System Elements, Architecture Graph, and Operational Mechanism (PE, PG, PH)

architectural graph and fail-closed decision flow depicted in Fig. 1 (PG):
- Module [1] User / Web UI: Captures station selection on a TWD97 map, activity
  type, duration, sensitivity group, and candidate time window.
- Module [2] Query Interpreter: Parses natural language into the canonical
  query.schema.json contract (Asia/Taipei display, UTC internal storage) and
  requests clarification if required fields are missing or ambiguous.
- Module [3] Constrained Planner Agent: Executes a ReAct loop restricted to an
  eight-tool allowlist (PH): list_stations, get_official_aqi, get_pm25_history,
  forecast_pm25, get_official_guidance, compare_windows, estimate_exposure_index,
  and verify_output.
- Module [4] MOENV Data Acquisition & Quality Gate: Retrieves live or snapshot
  records, enforcing five formal data contracts (Observation, OfficialAQI,
  Forecast, ActivityWindow, AgentResponse) and checking three distinct
  timestamps (observed_at_utc, published_at_utc, retrieved_at_utc).
- Module [5] PM2.5 Forecast & Analytics Engine: Computes causal lag/rolling
  features at origin t, produces h in {1, 3, 6} h predictions from frozen
  models, attaches validation error bands, and supports P1 SHAP attributions.
- Module [6] Versioned Official AQI Domain Rules: Applies Taiwan MOENV's 2025
  six-tier AQI breakpoints and differentiated activity guidance for general vs.
  sensitive groups [11].
- Module [7] Conservative Activity Window Comparator: Evaluates candidate
  intervals within 6 hours, computes the station-scale concentration-time
  exposure index, and abstains from ranking statistically indistinguishable
  windows.
- Module [8] Evidence-Grounded Output & Audit Logger: Validates the payload
  against report.schema.json, rendering separate cards for Official AQI,
  Predicted PM2.5, and Station Exposure Index with citations, disclaimers, and a
  redacted trace_id.

C. Mathematical Formulations and Algorithmic Implementation (PF, PI, PM)
Multi-Horizon Forecasting and Skill Formulation (PF): Let y_{s,t} denote the raw
hourly observed PM2.5 concentration (ug/m^3) at station s and discrete hour t.
For horizons h in {1, 3, 6} h, the causal feature vector x_{s,t} uses only
observations available at origin t: lags l in {1, 3, 6, 12, 24} h, backward
rolling statistics (mean, standard deviation, min, max over [t-w+1, t] for
w in {3, 6, 24} h), cyclical hour/weekday encodings, missingness flags, and
verified co-located wind vectors. Candidate ML models (Ridge, Random Forest,
XGBoost) are benchmarked against Persistence and 24-hour Seasonal Naive
baselines on the exact same valid sample pairs N_{s,h}:

   y_hat_pers(t + h | t) = y(t),   y_hat_snaive(t + h | t) = y(t + h - 24)  (1)

   MAE_{s,h}  = (1 / |N_{s,h}|) * sum_{t in N_{s,h}} |y_{s,t+h} - y_hat_{s,t+h|t}|   (2)

   RMSE_{s,h} = sqrt( (1 / |N_{s,h}|) * sum_{t in N_{s,h}} (y_{s,t+h} - y_hat_{s,t+h|t})^2 ) (3)

   Skill_MAE(s, h) = 1 - ( MAE_model(s, h) / MAE_pers(s, h) )               (4)

Station-Scale Exposure Proxy and Uncertainty Abstention Bound (PF, PM): For a
candidate activity window W partitioned into sub-intervals i of duration
Delta_t_i (hours) with verified station or forecasted concentration C_s(t_i)
(ug/m^3), the station-scale concentration-time exposure index is defined as:

   ExposureIndex(W) = sum_{i in W} C_s(t_i) * Delta_t_i    [ug*h/m^3]       (5)

To guarantee deterministic decision safety (PM), let C_bar(W) =
ExposureIndex(W) / sum_{i in W} Delta_t_i denote the window-mean predicted
concentration and tau_err(s, h) = MAE_valid(s, h) denote the validation error
margin. When comparing two candidate windows W_1 and W_2, the comparator
enforces the abstention bound:

   |C_bar(W_1) - C_bar(W_2)| < tau_err(s, h)  ==>  Decision = Abstain_Rank  (6)

Equation (6) mathematically prevents the agent from issuing false high-confidence
rankings when inter-window differences lie within model error noise.
Furthermore, if any sub-interval i in W has quality_flag != ok, ExposureIndex(W)
is flagged as partial/unknown rather than substituting 0 ug/m^3. Algorithms 1
and 2 (PI) formalize the leakage-safe training pipeline and the runtime
evidence-grounded agent loop.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Algorithm 1: Leakage-Safe Multi-Horizon Training & Backtesting (PI)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input : Station hourly data D_raw, horizons H={1,3,6}, split (70/15/15)
Output: Frozen versioned models M_frozen, evaluation report R_eval
01: D <- StandardizeUnitsAndTimezones(D_raw, unit="ug/m^3", tz="UTC")
02: D <- ReconcileDuplicatesAndQC(D, policy="dedup_identical", max=1000.0)
03: D <- SortChronologicallyByStationAndTime(D)
04: for each station s in Stations(D) do
05:   for each horizon h in H do
06:     (X_sh, y_sh, mask) <- BuildCausalLagRollingPairs(D_s, cutoff=t)
07:     (Train, Valid, Test) <- ChronologicalSplit(X_sh, y_sh, mask)
08:     p_pers <- PredictPersistence(Valid, Test, h)
09:     p_snaive <- PredictSeasonalNaive24h(Valid, Test, h)
10:     Candidates <- {Ridge, RandomForest, XGBoost}
11:     TuneOnRollingOrigin(Candidates, Train, Valid, fit_on_train_only=true)
12:     m_best <- SelectByValidMAEAndLatency(Candidates, p_pers, p_snaive)
13:     FreezeModel(m_best, sha256=Hash(D_s)); p_test <- PredictOnce(m_best, Test)
14:     CompareToBaselines(p_test, p_pers, p_snaive, metrics={MAE,RMSE,Skill})
15:     StratifyByStationHorizonAndPollutionEpisode(R_eval[s,h])
16:     CalibrateResidualErrorBands(m_best, Valid)
17:     AnnotateMode(R_eval[s,h], mode="reconstructed_from_observed_at")
18:   end for
19: end for
20: return M_frozen, R_eval
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Algorithm 2: Evidence-Grounded AirGuard Decision Agent (Runtime, PI)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input : User query Q, context C_ctx, allowlisted tools T_allow
Output: Verified AgentResponse R_out with audit trace
01: req <- ParseIntent(Q, C_ctx, tz="Asia/Taipei")
02: if MissingRequiredFields(req) then return AskClarification(req)
03: station <- InvokeTool(T_allow.list_stations, req.location)
04: if station is ambiguous then return ListCandidateStations(req.location)
05: if not ValidateHorizon(req.time_window, max_h=6) then
06:   return OutOfHorizonResponse(limit="6 hours", official_link=MOENV_URL)
07: end if
08: live <- InvokeTool(T_allow.get_official_aqi, station.id)
09: hist <- InvokeTool(T_allow.get_pm25_history, station.id, hours=24)
10: qc <- ValidateTimestampUnitFreshnessAndCoverage(live, hist)
11: if qc.is_stale or qc.is_insufficient then
12:   return SafeFallback(status="partial_or_unavailable", qc, live)
13: end if
14: latest_aqi <- live.official_aqi  // Strictly NOT model output
15: forecasts <- InvokeTool(T_allow.forecast_pm25, station.id, H={1,3,6})
16: guidance <- InvokeTool(T_allow.get_official_guidance, latest_aqi, req.group)
17: windows <- InvokeTool(T_allow.compare_windows, req, forecasts, guidance)
18: exposure <- InvokeTool(T_allow.estimate_exposure_index, windows, req.dur)
19: R_draft <- ComposeSeparateCards(latest_aqi, forecasts, exposure, guidance)
20: if not InvokeTool(T_allow.verify_output, R_draft).passed then
21:   R_draft <- SafeFallback(status="partial", qc, live)
22: end if
23: LogRedactedAuditTrace(req, R_draft); return R_draft

IV. EXPERIMENT

Following the Q-V experimental evaluation methodology, we present our
quantitative metrics, baseline comparisons, Taichung 6-station simulation setup,
quality-control tuning parameters, completed Phase W1 empirical audit results,
and dual-track verification matrix.

A. Quality and Quantity Metrics and Related Methods to Compare (Q, R)
Performance Metrics (Q): AirGuard AI is evaluated along two orthogonal tracks:
1) Track A (Multi-Horizon PM2.5 Forecasting & Decision Utility): Quantifies point
prediction accuracy via MAE_{s,h} (Eq. 2), RMSE_{s,h} (Eq. 3), and
Skill_MAE(s, h) (Eq. 4) across h in {1, 3, 6} h, supplemented by Decision
Regret—defined as the station-scale ExposureIndex difference (ug*h/m^3) between
the model-recommended window and the ex-post minimum-concentration observed
window—and the uncertainty-overlap abstention rate (Eq. 6).
2) Track B (Domain AI Agent & Guardrail Reliability): Quantifies end-to-end
decision quality across 30 fixed scenarios using five metrics: Tool Selection
Accuracy (%), Provenance & Citation Completeness Rate (%), 2025 MOENV Rule
Consistency Rate (%), Fail-Closed Safe Degradation Rate (%), and End-to-End
Response Latency (Median P50 and P95 in ms).

Related Methods to Compare (R): In Track A, we compare two parameter-free
baselines (Persistence and 24-Hour Seasonal Naive) against three classical ML
models (Ridge Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost) on identical valid sample
pairs. In Track B, we compare a deterministic Rule-Only baseline (using only
current official AQI without short-term ML forecasts or ReAct query
disambiguation) against the full AirGuard AI (Agent + ML + Domain Rules)
pipeline under identical data snapshots.

B. Simulation Setup and Quality-Control Parameter Tuning (S, T)
Simulation Setup (S): We select all six official MOENV monitoring stations in
Taichung City as our empirical testbed: five general ambient stations—Fengyuan
(28), Shalu (29), Dali (30), Zhongming (31), and Xitun (32, primary pilot
station)—and one traffic station, Taiwan Avenue (88). Each station's hourly
PM2.5 series from AQX_P_13 is split chronologically into 70% training, 15%
validation, and 15% held-out test partitions, with rolling-origin evaluation on
the development split and a single locked evaluation on the test split. For
Track B, a fixed casebook of 30 test scenarios (6 categories B1-B6 x 5 cases)
probes normal scheduling, sensitive groups, spatiotemporal ambiguity,
missing/stale data, prompt injection, and AQI/PM2.5 semantic separation.

Parameter Tuning and Quality-Control Thresholds (T): Feature engineering is
tuned over causal lag windows l in {1, 3, 6, 12, 24} h and backward rolling
windows w in {3, 6, 24} h, with preprocessors fitted strictly on each training
fold. Four critical data quality parameters were calibrated during Phase W1:
- Duplicate Policy: Set to on_duplicate = "dedup_identical", which deduplicates
  repeated daily JSON objects only when all 24 hourly values and quality flags
  match identically, throwing a DataError on any value conflict.
- Physical Instrument QC Ceiling: Set to MAX_VALID_PM25_UG_M3 = 1000.0 ug/m^3
  (reflecting the physical measurement limit of MOENV beta-attenuation/FEM
  monitors rather than clipping at the AQI 500 breakpoint of 500.4 ug/m^3).
- Trainability Thresholds: A station-month is marked is_calendar_month_trainable
  iff calendar_coverage >= 80% and valid_hours >= 336, and is_window_trainable
  iff window_coverage >= 80% and valid_hours >= 336.
- Backtest Provenance Mode: Tagged as reconstructed_from_observed_at because
  AQX_P_13 and AQX_P_488 omit historical publication-delay timestamps
  (published_at_utc = null).

V. CONCLUSION

A. What Proposed and Methodological Excellence (W, X)
What Proposed (W): To address the disconnect between static air quality
monitoring dashboards and trustworthy daily outdoor activity scheduling, this
paper proposed AirGuard AI, an evidence-grounded Domain AI Agent integrating
Taiwan MOENV open data, leakage-safe short-term PM2.5 forecasting, versioned
regulatory rules, and fail-closed decision support.

Methodological Excellence (X): AirGuard AI excels in station-scale, short-
horizon (1-6 h) outdoor activity decision scenarios by enforcing a strict three-
tier separation of concerns: an eight-tool allowlisted ReAct planner agent
parses user intent without fabricating numbers, a causal rolling-origin ML
pipeline benchmarks Ridge, Random Forest, and XGBoost against Persistence and
24-hour Seasonal Naive baselines, and a deterministic rule comparator separates
official multi-pollutant AQI from predicted PM2.5 while abstaining when
candidate windows fall within validation error bounds.

B. Empirical Yields, Scope Limitations, and Future Work (Y, Z)
Empirical Yields (Y): Our completed Phase W1 empirical audit across all six
official MOENV stations in Taichung City verified 123,055 valid hourly PM2.5
observations (2024-2026) with 95.49%-97.90% window coverage and cryptographic
SHA256 offline reproducibility, while identifying and resolving upstream
duplicate daily rows, unobserved calendar days, and pagination timeout limits.

Limitations and Future Work (Z): Because fixed regulatory stations cannot
capture street-canyon microenvironments or individual physiological ventilation,
AirGuard AI explicitly excludes street-level clean-route navigation, personal
inhaled microgram dose claims, and clinical medical diagnosis. In ongoing work
across Phases W2-W6, we are executing the pre-registered Track A multi-horizon
backtests and Track B 30-scenario agent evaluations, integrating P1 SHAP tree
explainability and multi-activity exposure logging, and deploying the open-
source FastAPI/Next.js decision interface.
